Light – Reflection and Refraction

Laws of reflection, spherical mirrors and the mirror formula, refraction and refractive index, spherical lenses, the lens formula, magnification and power of a lens.

Reflection of light

Light travels in straight lines. When it falls on a polished surface such as a mirror, it bounces back. This is reflection.

Laws of reflection

(1) The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection: \( \angle i = \angle r \).

(2) The incident ray, the normal to the mirror at the point of incidence, and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.

These laws apply to all reflecting surfaces, including curved ones. The image in a plane mirror is virtual, erect, the same size as the object, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, and laterally inverted.

Spherical mirrors

A spherical mirror is part of a hollow sphere. A concave mirror has its reflecting surface curved inwards (converging mirror). A convex mirror has its reflecting surface curved outwards (diverging mirror).

for mirrors of small aperture

Rules for drawing ray diagrams

  1. A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through F after reflection (concave), or appears to diverge from F (convex).
  2. A ray through F (concave), or directed towards F (convex), becomes parallel to the principal axis after reflection.
  3. A ray through C (concave), or directed towards C (convex), is reflected back along the same path.
  4. A ray striking the pole at an angle is reflected at an equal angle on the other side of the principal axis.

Uses of concave mirrors: torches, car headlights and searchlights (to get a strong parallel beam); shaving and make-up mirrors (enlarged, erect image); dentists’ mirrors; solar furnaces (to concentrate sunlight).

New Cartesian sign convention

  • The object is always placed to the left of the mirror or lens, so light falls from left to right.
  • All distances are measured from the pole (mirror) or optical centre (lens).
  • Distances measured in the direction of incident light (to the right) are positive; against it (to the left) are negative.
  • Heights measured upwards from the principal axis are positive; downwards are negative.
Remember

Object distance \(u\) is always negative. Concave mirror: \(f\) negative. Convex mirror: \(f\) positive. Convex lens: \(f\) positive. Concave lens: \(f\) negative. A negative magnification means the image is real and inverted; a positive one means virtual and erect.

Mirror formula and magnification

Mirror formula
\( \dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f} \)
u = object distance, v = image distance
Magnification (mirror)
\( m = \dfrac{h'}{h} = -\dfrac{v}{u} \)
no unit; |m| > 1 means enlarged

Refraction of light

When light travels obliquely from one transparent medium to another, its direction changes at the boundary. This bending is called refraction. It happens because the speed of light is different in different media. Examples: a pencil partly in water looks bent; a coin at the bottom of a bucket appears raised; letters seen through a glass slab appear raised.

  • Light going from a rarer to a denser medium (air → glass) slows down and bends towards the normal.
  • Light going from a denser to a rarer medium (glass → air) speeds up and bends away from the normal.
  • A ray falling along the normal (\( \angle i = 0 \)) goes straight through without bending.
Laws of refraction

(1) The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

(2) Snell’s law: for light of a given colour and a given pair of media, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant.

Snell’s law
\( \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r} = n_{21} \)
refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1
Absolute refractive index
\( n = \dfrac{c}{v} \)
c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (speed in vacuum); no unit
Relative refractive index
\( n_{21} = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1} = \dfrac{v_1}{v_2} \)
medium 2 with respect to medium 1

Some refractive indices: water 1.33, kerosene 1.44, crown glass 1.52, diamond 2.42 (the highest among common materials). A medium with a higher refractive index is optically denser; optical density is not the same as mass density.

Refraction through a rectangular glass slab

The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but shifted sideways. This sideways shift is called lateral displacement. The angle of incidence equals the angle of emergence, because the bending at the two parallel faces is equal and opposite.

Spherical lenses

A convex lens is thicker at the middle than at the edges and converges light (converging lens). A concave lens is thinner at the middle and diverges light (diverging lens). The centre of a lens is its optical centre (O); a ray through O passes without deviation. A lens has two principal foci, F₁ and F₂, one on each side.

Lens formula
\( \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f} \)
note the minus sign (mirror formula has plus)

\( m = \dfrac{h'}{h} = \dfrac{v}{u} \)

Power of a lens

Definition

The power of a lens is its ability to converge or diverge light rays. It is the reciprocal of the focal length in metres.

Power
\( P = \dfrac{1}{f\ (\text{in m})} \)
SI unit: dioptre (D) = m⁻¹
Lenses in contact
\( P = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots \)
powers simply add

1 dioptre is the power of a lens of focal length 1 m. The power of a convex lens is positive; the power of a concave lens is negative. An optician’s prescription of +2.0 D means a convex lens of focal length 0.5 m.

Solved examples

An object 4 cm tall is placed 25 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.

\( u = -25\ \text{cm},\ f = -15\ \text{cm},\ h = +4\ \text{cm} \)

\( \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = -\dfrac{1}{15} + \dfrac{1}{25} = \dfrac{-5 + 3}{75} = -\dfrac{2}{75} \Rightarrow v = -37.5\ \text{cm} \)

\( m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{-37.5}{-25} = -1.5 \), so \( h' = mh = -6\ \text{cm} \)

Image is 37.5 cm in front of the mirror, 6 cm tall, real, inverted and enlarged.

A convex mirror used as a rear-view mirror has a radius of curvature of 3 m. A bus is 5 m behind it. Find the position, nature and magnification of the image.

\( R = +3\ \text{m} \Rightarrow f = +1.5\ \text{m};\ u = -5\ \text{m} \)

\( \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{1.5} + \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{10 + 3}{15} = \dfrac{13}{15} \Rightarrow v = +1.15\ \text{m} \)

\( m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{1.15}{-5} = +0.23 \)

Image is 1.15 m behind the mirror, virtual, erect and diminished (0.23 times the size).

Light enters glass of refractive index 1.50 from air. Find the speed of light in glass. (\( c = 3 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s} \))

\( v = \dfrac{c}{n} = \dfrac{3 \times 10^{8}}{1.50} = 2 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m/s} \)

Speed in glass = \( 2 \times 10^{8} \) m/s.

A 2 cm tall object is placed 15 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.

\( u = -15\ \text{cm},\ f = +10\ \text{cm} \)

\( \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{3 - 2}{30} = \dfrac{1}{30} \Rightarrow v = +30\ \text{cm} \)

\( m = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{30}{-15} = -2 \), so \( h' = -4\ \text{cm} \)

Image is 30 cm on the other side of the lens, 4 cm tall, real, inverted and enlarged.

A concave lens of focal length 15 cm forms an image 10 cm from the lens. How far is the object from the lens?

A concave lens forms a virtual image on the object’s side: \( v = -10\ \text{cm},\ f = -15\ \text{cm} \)

\( \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{f} = -\dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{-3 + 2}{30} = -\dfrac{1}{30} \Rightarrow u = -30\ \text{cm} \)

The object is 30 cm from the lens.

Find the power of a concave lens of focal length 2 m.

\( f = -2\ \text{m} \Rightarrow P = \dfrac{1}{-2} = -0.5\ \text{D} \)

Power = −0.5 D.

Quick revision

  • Reflection: \( \angle i = \angle r \); incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in one plane.
  • \( R = 2f \). Concave mirror converges (f negative); convex mirror diverges (f positive).
  • Mirror: \( \tfrac1v + \tfrac1u = \tfrac1f \), \( m = -v/u \). Lens: \( \tfrac1v - \tfrac1u = \tfrac1f \), \( m = v/u \).
  • Object distance u is always negative in the New Cartesian sign convention.
  • Snell’s law: \( \sin i / \sin r = \) constant; \( n = c/v \).
  • Rarer → denser: bends towards the normal. Glass slab: emergent ray parallel to incident ray (lateral displacement).
  • Convex lens converges (f and P positive); concave lens diverges (f and P negative).
  • Power \( P = 1/f \) (f in metres), unit dioptre (D). Powers of lenses in contact add.
  • Convex mirror and concave lens always give a virtual, erect, diminished image.
