Gravitation

The universal law of gravitation, free fall and g, mass and weight, thrust and pressure, buoyancy, Archimedes’ principle and relative density.

Universal law of gravitation

Newton’s law of gravitation

Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres. The force acts along the line joining the two centres.

\[ F \propto m_1 m_2, \qquad F \propto \frac{1}{d^2} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad F = G\,\frac{m_1 m_2}{d^2} \]
Gravitational force
\( F = \dfrac{G\,m_1 m_2}{d^2} \)
F in N, m in kg, d in m
Universal gravitational constant
\( G = 6.673 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\,\text{kg}^{-2} \)
Measured by Henry Cavendish

It is called the inverse-square law: if the distance is doubled, the force becomes one-fourth.

Importance of the law: it explains the force that binds us to the Earth, the motion of the Moon around the Earth, the motion of planets around the Sun, and the tides caused by the Moon and the Sun.

Free fall and acceleration due to gravity

When an object falls towards the Earth under gravity alone, it is in free fall. Its velocity increases at a constant rate called the acceleration due to gravity, \(g\).

Acceleration due to gravity
\( g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2} \)
M = mass of Earth, R = radius of Earth
Value near Earth’s surface
\( g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \)
\( M = 6 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg},\ R = 6.4 \times 10^{6}\ \text{m} \)
  • \(g\) does not depend on the mass of the falling object. A feather and a coin fall together in a vacuum; in air, the feather falls slowly because of air resistance.
  • The Earth is not a perfect sphere: its radius is larger at the equator than at the poles, so \(g\) is greater at the poles.
  • \(g\) decreases with height above the surface.

The equations of motion apply to free fall with \(a\) replaced by \(g\): take \( g = +9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \) for a falling object and \( g = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \) for an object thrown upwards.

\( v = u + gt \)
\( h = ut + \tfrac12 gt^2 \)
\( v^2 - u^2 = 2gh \)

Thrust and pressure

Definitions

Thrust is the force acting on an object perpendicular to its surface. Unit: newton (N).

Pressure is the thrust acting per unit area.

Pressure
\( P = \dfrac{\text{thrust}}{\text{area}} = \dfrac{F}{A} \)
SI unit: pascal (Pa) = N/m²

The same force produces more pressure on a smaller area. That is why a knife has a sharp edge, nails and pins have pointed tips, and school bags, buildings and heavy vehicles have wide straps, foundations or tyres (to reduce pressure).

Pressure in fluids: liquids and gases exert pressure in all directions on the walls of the container and on objects placed in them.

Buoyancy and Archimedes’ principle

When an object is placed in a fluid, the fluid pushes it upwards. This upward force is called the buoyant force or upthrust, and the tendency is called buoyancy. The buoyant force depends on the density of the fluid.

Archimedes’ principle

When a body is wholly or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

Why objects float or sink

  • If the density of the object is less than that of the liquid, it floats (a cork on water).
  • If the density of the object is more than that of the liquid, it sinks (an iron nail in water).

Applications: designing ships and submarines, lactometers (to test the purity of milk) and hydrometers (to measure the density of liquids).

Density and relative density

Density
\( \rho = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} \)
SI unit: kg/m³
Relative density
\( \text{R.D.} = \dfrac{\text{density of substance}}{\text{density of water}} \)
No unit (ratio). Density of water = 1000 kg/m³

Solved examples

The mass of the Earth is \( 6 \times 10^{24} \) kg and that of the Moon is \( 7.4 \times 10^{22} \) kg. The distance between them is \( 3.84 \times 10^{5} \) km. Find the gravitational force between them. (\( G = 6.7 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2} \))

\( d = 3.84 \times 10^{8}\ \text{m} \)

\[ F = \frac{6.7 \times 10^{-11} \times 6 \times 10^{24} \times 7.4 \times 10^{22}}{(3.84 \times 10^{8})^2} \]

\( F \approx 2.01 \times 10^{20}\ \text{N} \)

A stone is dropped from the top of a tower and reaches the ground in 4 s. Find the speed with which it hits the ground and the height of the tower. (\( g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \))

\( u = 0,\ t = 4\ \text{s} \)

\( v = u + gt = 9.8 \times 4 = 39.2\ \text{m/s} \)

\( h = \tfrac12 g t^2 = \tfrac12 \times 9.8 \times 16 = 78.4\ \text{m} \)

Speed = 39.2 m/s; height = 78.4 m.

A ball is thrown vertically upwards with a velocity of 49 m/s. Find the maximum height it reaches and the total time it takes to return to the thrower.

At the highest point \( v = 0 \); \( g = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \).

\( v^2 - u^2 = 2gh \Rightarrow 0 - 49^2 = 2(-9.8)h \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{2401}{19.6} = 122.5\ \text{m} \)

Time to go up: \( 0 = 49 - 9.8t \Rightarrow t = 5\ \text{s} \). It takes the same time to come down.

Maximum height = 122.5 m; total time = 10 s.

An object has a mass of 10 kg. Find its weight on the Earth and on the Moon.

On Earth: \( W = mg = 10 \times 9.8 = 98\ \text{N} \)

On Moon: \( W = \tfrac16 \times 98 = 16.3\ \text{N} \)

98 N on the Earth, 16.3 N on the Moon. Its mass is 10 kg in both places.

A wooden block of mass 5 kg measures 40 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm. Find the pressure it exerts on a table when it rests on (a) its 20 cm × 10 cm face and (b) its 40 cm × 20 cm face. (\( g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \))

Thrust = weight \( = 5 \times 9.8 = 49\ \text{N} \)

(a) \( A = 0.20 \times 0.10 = 0.02\ \text{m}^2 \); \( P = \dfrac{49}{0.02} = 2450\ \text{Pa} \)

(b) \( A = 0.40 \times 0.20 = 0.08\ \text{m}^2 \); \( P = \dfrac{49}{0.08} = 612.5\ \text{Pa} \)

The same thrust gives more pressure on the smaller face.

The density of silver is \( 10.8 \times 10^{3}\ \text{kg/m}^3 \). Find its relative density.

\( \text{R.D.} = \dfrac{10.8 \times 10^{3}}{1 \times 10^{3}} = 10.8 \)

Relative density of silver = 10.8 (no unit).

Quick revision

  • \( F = Gm_1m_2/d^2 \); \( G = 6.673 \times 10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2} \) (universal constant).
  • \( g = GM/R^2 = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \); independent of the mass of the falling body; greater at the poles.
  • For free fall use the equations of motion with \( a = g \) (negative when going up).
  • Mass is constant (kg); weight \( W = mg \) varies (N). Weight on the Moon = 1/6 of weight on the Earth.
  • Pressure \( P = F/A \), unit pascal (N/m²). Smaller area → greater pressure.
  • Archimedes’ principle: upthrust = weight of fluid displaced.
  • Object floats if its density is less than the liquid’s; sinks if more.
  • Relative density = density of substance ÷ density of water (no unit).
