Force and Laws of Motion

Balanced and unbalanced forces, inertia, Newton’s three laws of motion, momentum and the law of conservation of momentum.

Force

Definition

A force is a push or a pull on an object. A force can make a stationary object move, stop a moving object, change its speed, change its direction of motion, or change its shape.

Force is a vector quantity. Its SI unit is the newton (N).

Balanced and unbalanced forces

  • Balanced forces: forces acting on an object whose resultant is zero. They do not change the state of rest or of uniform motion, though they may change the shape. Example: two teams pulling a rope with equal force in a tug-of-war.
  • Unbalanced forces: forces whose resultant is not zero. An unbalanced force changes the speed or direction of motion, that is, it produces acceleration.

When we push a heavy box and it does not move, the friction force balances our push. Only when our push exceeds friction does the box start moving.

Newton’s first law of motion

First law

An object remains in a state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled to change that state by an applied (unbalanced) external force.

The first law is also called the law of inertia.

Inertia

Inertia is the natural tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or of uniform motion. Mass is a measure of inertia: a heavier object has more inertia. That is why it is harder to push a loaded truck than an empty cart.

Type of inertiaEveryday example
Inertia of restPassengers fall backwards when a bus starts suddenly. Dust comes out of a carpet when it is beaten. A coin on a card falls into the glass when the card is flicked away.
Inertia of motionPassengers fall forwards when a moving bus stops suddenly. An athlete keeps running for a few steps after crossing the finish line.
Inertia of directionPassengers lean outwards when a car takes a sharp turn.

Momentum

Definition

The momentum \(p\) of an object is the product of its mass \(m\) and velocity \(v\). It has the same direction as the velocity.

Momentum
\( p = mv \)
SI unit: kg m/s (kg m s⁻¹) · vector

A bullet of small mass can be deadly because of its very high velocity, and a slow truck can be dangerous because of its very large mass. In both cases the momentum is large.

Newton’s second law of motion

Second law

The rate of change of momentum of an object is proportional to the applied unbalanced force, and takes place in the direction of the force.

Mathematical form

Let a force \(F\) change the velocity of mass \(m\) from \(u\) to \(v\) in time \(t\).

\[ F \propto \frac{mv - mu}{t} = m\,\frac{v - u}{t} = ma \]

So \( F = kma \). The unit of force is chosen so that \( k = 1 \), giving

Second law
\( F = ma = \dfrac{m(v - u)}{t} \)
1 N = 1 kg × 1 m/s² = 1 kg m s⁻²
One newton

1 newton is the force that produces an acceleration of 1 m/s² in a body of mass 1 kg.

Applications: a fielder pulls his hands back while catching a fast cricket ball. This increases the time taken to stop the ball, so the rate of change of momentum, and therefore the force on his hands, becomes smaller. For the same reason high jumpers land on soft cushions or sand, and cars have crumple zones and airbags.

Newton’s third law of motion

Third law

To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

  • Action and reaction are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  • They always act on two different objects, so they never cancel each other.
  • They act at the same instant.

Examples: a gun recoils backwards when a bullet is fired; we walk by pushing the ground backwards and the ground pushes us forwards; a boat moves backwards when a sailor jumps forwards onto the shore; a rocket moves up by pushing hot gases down.

Law of conservation of momentum

Law

When two (or more) objects interact, their total momentum remains conserved, provided no external unbalanced force acts on them.

Consider two balls A and B, of masses \(m_A\) and \(m_B\), moving with velocities \(u_A\) and \(u_B\). They collide for a time \(t\), and their velocities become \(v_A\) and \(v_B\). By the third law, the force on A by B equals minus the force on B by A:

\[ \frac{m_A(v_A - u_A)}{t} = -\,\frac{m_B(v_B - u_B)}{t} \]
Conservation of momentum
\( m_Au_A + m_Bu_B = m_Av_A + m_Bv_B \)
total momentum before = total momentum after
Recoil velocity of a gun
\( v_g = -\dfrac{m_b\,v_b}{m_g} \)
b = bullet, g = gun; both start at rest

Solved examples

A force of 5 N gives a mass \(m_1\) an acceleration of 10 m/s² and a mass \(m_2\) an acceleration of 20 m/s². What acceleration would it give if both masses were tied together?

\( m_1 = \dfrac{F}{a_1} = \dfrac{5}{10} = 0.5\ \text{kg} \), \( m_2 = \dfrac{5}{20} = 0.25\ \text{kg} \)

Total mass \( = 0.75\ \text{kg} \). \( a = \dfrac{5}{0.75} = 6.67\ \text{m/s}^2 \)

Acceleration = 6.67 m/s².

A car of mass 1000 kg moving at 72 km/h is brought to rest in 4 s. Find the change in momentum and the braking force.

\( u = 72 \times \tfrac{5}{18} = 20\ \text{m/s},\ v = 0 \)

Change in momentum \( = m(v - u) = 1000(0 - 20) = -20\,000\ \text{kg m/s} \)

\( F = \dfrac{-20\,000}{4} = -5000\ \text{N} \)

Braking force = 5000 N, acting opposite to the motion.

A fielder stops a cricket ball of mass 150 g moving at 20 m/s in 0.1 s. What force does the ball exert on his hands? What happens if he stops it in 0.5 s instead?

\( m = 0.15\ \text{kg} \). Change in momentum \( = 0.15 \times 20 = 3\ \text{kg m/s} \)

In 0.1 s: \( F = \dfrac{3}{0.1} = 30\ \text{N} \). In 0.5 s: \( F = \dfrac{3}{0.5} = 6\ \text{N} \)

30 N, which falls to 6 N when he pulls his hands back and takes longer to stop the ball.

A bullet of mass 20 g is fired horizontally at 150 m/s from a pistol of mass 2 kg. Find the recoil velocity of the pistol.

Before firing, total momentum = 0.

\( 0 = (0.02)(150) + (2)\,v_g \Rightarrow v_g = -\dfrac{3}{2} = -1.5\ \text{m/s} \)

The pistol recoils at 1.5 m/s, opposite to the bullet.

Two objects of masses 100 g and 200 g move along the same line and direction with velocities 2 m/s and 1 m/s. They collide, and after the collision the first object moves at 1.67 m/s. Find the velocity of the second object.

Total momentum before \( = 0.1 \times 2 + 0.2 \times 1 = 0.4\ \text{kg m/s} \)

After: \( 0.1 \times 1.67 + 0.2\,v_2 = 0.4 \Rightarrow 0.2\,v_2 = 0.233 \Rightarrow v_2 = 1.17\ \text{m/s} \)

The second object moves at 1.17 m/s in the same direction.

Quick revision

  • Balanced forces: resultant zero, no acceleration. Unbalanced forces: produce acceleration.
  • First law = law of inertia. Mass is the measure of inertia.
  • Momentum \( p = mv \), unit kg m/s, vector.
  • Second law: \( F = ma = m(v - u)/t \). 1 N = 1 kg m/s².
  • Increasing the stopping time reduces the force (catching a ball, cushions, airbags).
  • Third law: action and reaction are equal, opposite, and act on different bodies.
  • Conservation of momentum: \( m_Au_A + m_Bu_B = m_Av_A + m_Bv_B \) when no external force acts.
  • Recoil of gun: \( m_g v_g = -\,m_b v_b \).
