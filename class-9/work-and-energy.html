Work and Energy

Scientific meaning of work, kinetic and potential energy, conservation of energy, power and the commercial unit of energy (kWh).

Work

Definition

Work is done when a force acts on an object and the object is displaced in the direction of the force. Work done = force × displacement in the direction of the force.

Work done
\( W = F \times s \)
SI unit: joule (J) = N m · scalar
One joule

1 joule is the work done when a force of 1 N displaces an object by 1 m in the direction of the force.

Two conditions must both be met for work to be done: (1) a force must act, and (2) the object must be displaced. A person pushing a wall that does not move does no work in the scientific sense, even if they get tired.

Positive, negative and zero work

  • Positive work: force and displacement are in the same direction. Example: pulling a trolley forwards.
  • Negative work: force is opposite to the displacement. Example: friction on a sliding box; the braking force on a car.
  • Zero work: force is perpendicular to the displacement, or there is no displacement. Example: a porter carrying a load on his head while walking on a level road does no work against gravity; gravity does no work on a satellite in a circular orbit.

Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work. An object that has energy can do work on another object. Energy has the same unit as work, the joule (J); a larger unit is the kilojoule (1 kJ = 1000 J).

Forms of energy include mechanical (kinetic + potential), heat, chemical, electrical, light and sound energy.

Kinetic energy

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because of its motion. A moving hammer drives a nail; flowing water turns a turbine.

Derivation

Let a force \(F\) accelerate an object of mass \(m\) from rest (\(u = 0\)) to velocity \(v\) over a displacement \(s\). From \( v^2 - u^2 = 2as \), \( s = \dfrac{v^2}{2a} \). So

\[ W = F \times s = ma \times \frac{v^2}{2a} = \tfrac12 mv^2 \]

This work is stored in the object as its kinetic energy.

Kinetic energy
\( E_k = \tfrac12 mv^2 \)
joule (J)
Work–energy relation
\( W = \tfrac12 mv^2 - \tfrac12 mu^2 \)
work done = change in kinetic energy

If the velocity is doubled, the kinetic energy becomes four times.

Potential energy

Potential energy is the energy stored in an object because of its position or shape (configuration). Examples: water stored in a dam, a stretched rubber band, a wound-up spring in a toy car, a raised hammer.

Potential energy at a height

To raise an object of mass \(m\) through height \(h\), a force equal to its weight \(mg\) acts through a distance \(h\). Work done \( = mg \times h \), and this is stored as gravitational potential energy.

Gravitational potential energy
\( E_p = mgh \)
joule (J); h measured from a chosen reference level

The work done against gravity depends only on the difference in height between the initial and final positions, not on the path taken.

Law of conservation of energy

Law

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be converted from one form to another. The total energy before and after any transformation remains the same.

Freely falling body: at the top, all its mechanical energy is potential energy. As it falls, potential energy decreases and kinetic energy increases by the same amount. Just before it hits the ground, all the energy is kinetic. At every point,

\[ mgh + \tfrac12 mv^2 = \text{constant} \]

Energy transformations: in a hydroelectric plant, potential energy of water → kinetic energy → electrical energy; in a bulb, electrical → light + heat; in green plants, light → chemical energy.

Power

Definition

Power is the rate of doing work, or the rate of transfer of energy.

Power
\( P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{E}{t} \)
SI unit: watt (W) = J/s
Larger unit
\( 1\ \text{kW} = 1000\ \text{W} \)
1 W = 1 J/s

Average power = total energy used ÷ total time taken.

Commercial unit of energy

The joule is too small for household electricity bills, so energy is measured in kilowatt hours (kWh), commonly called units. 1 kWh is the energy used in one hour at the rate of 1 kW.

Kilowatt hour
\( 1\ \text{kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^{6}\ \text{J} \)
1000 J/s × 3600 s
Energy used
\( E\ (\text{kWh}) = P\ (\text{kW}) \times t\ (\text{h}) \)
1 kWh = 1 unit on the electricity bill

Solved examples

A force of 7 N acts on an object and displaces it by 8 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.

\( W = F \times s = 7 \times 8 = 56\ \text{J} \)

Work done = 56 J.

An object of mass 15 kg moves with a uniform velocity of 4 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?

\( E_k = \tfrac12 mv^2 = \tfrac12 \times 15 \times 4^2 = 120\ \text{J} \)

Kinetic energy = 120 J.

How much work is needed to increase the speed of a 1000 kg car from 36 km/h to 72 km/h?

\( u = 10\ \text{m/s},\ v = 20\ \text{m/s} \)

Initial KE \( = \tfrac12 \times 1000 \times 10^2 = 50\,000\ \text{J} \); final KE \( = \tfrac12 \times 1000 \times 20^2 = 200\,000\ \text{J} \)

Work = change in KE \( = 200\,000 - 50\,000 = 150\,000\ \text{J} \)

Work needed = \( 1.5 \times 10^{5}\ \text{J} \) = 150 kJ.

A ball of mass 2 kg is dropped from a height of 5 m. Find its potential energy at the top, and its kinetic energy and speed just before it hits the ground. (\( g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2 \))

PE at top \( = mgh = 2 \times 9.8 \times 5 = 98\ \text{J} \)

By conservation of energy, KE just before impact \( = 98\ \text{J} \).

\( \tfrac12 \times 2 \times v^2 = 98 \Rightarrow v^2 = 98 \Rightarrow v \approx 9.9\ \text{m/s} \)

PE = 98 J; KE = 98 J; speed ≈ 9.9 m/s.

A girl weighing 400 N climbs a vertical height of 8 m on a staircase in 20 s. Find the work done and her power.

\( W = mgh = 400 \times 8 = 3200\ \text{J} \)

\( P = \dfrac{W}{t} = \dfrac{3200}{20} = 160\ \text{W} \)

Work = 3200 J; power = 160 W.

A house uses four 60 W bulbs for 6 hours every day. Find the energy used in 30 days, and the cost at ₹5 per unit.

Energy per day \( = 4 \times 60 \times 6 = 1440\ \text{Wh} = 1.44\ \text{kWh} \)

In 30 days: \( 1.44 \times 30 = 43.2\ \text{kWh} \)

Cost = 43.2 × ₹5 = ₹216

43.2 units; cost = ₹216.

Quick revision

  • Work \( W = Fs \) (displacement along the force). Unit: joule. Scalar.
  • No displacement or force perpendicular to displacement → zero work. Force opposite to displacement → negative work.
  • Energy = capacity to do work; unit joule.
  • \( E_k = \tfrac12 mv^2 \); doubling v makes KE four times. Work done = change in KE.
  • \( E_p = mgh \); depends only on the height difference, not on the path.
  • Energy is never created or destroyed; in free fall, PE + KE stays constant.
  • Power \( P = W/t \), unit watt (J/s). 1 kW = 1000 W.
  • 1 kWh = 1 unit = \( 3.6 \times 10^6 \) J.
