Motion

Distance and displacement, speed and velocity, acceleration, motion graphs, the three equations of motion and uniform circular motion.

Rest and motion

An object is in motion when its position changes with time, and at rest when it does not. Rest and motion are relative: a passenger sitting in a moving bus is at rest with respect to the bus but in motion with respect to the trees outside. To describe a position we always need a reference point (the origin).

Distance and displacement

Definitions

Distance is the total length of the path travelled. It has only magnitude, so it is a scalar quantity.

Displacement is the shortest straight-line distance from the initial position to the final position, in a stated direction. It has magnitude and direction, so it is a vector quantity.

  • SI unit of both is the metre (m).
  • Distance is never negative. Displacement can be positive, negative or zero.
  • If an object returns to its starting point, its displacement is zero even though it has covered a distance.
  • Magnitude of displacement ≤ distance travelled.

Uniform and non-uniform motion

Uniform motion: equal distances are covered in equal intervals of time, however small the intervals. Example: a car moving at a steady 60 km/h on a straight road.

Non-uniform motion: unequal distances are covered in equal intervals of time. Example: a car moving in city traffic, a ball rolling down a slope.

Speed and velocity

Speed
\( \text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \)
SI unit: m/s (m s⁻¹) · scalar
Average speed
\( v_{\text{av}} = \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} \)
SI unit: m/s
Velocity
\( \vec v = \dfrac{\text{displacement}}{\text{time}} \)
SI unit: m/s · vector (speed in a given direction)
Average velocity (uniform change)
\( v_{\text{av}} = \dfrac{u + v}{2} \)
u = initial velocity, v = final velocity
Unit conversion

\( 1\ \text{km/h} = \dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{3600\ \text{s}} = \dfrac{5}{18}\ \text{m/s} \). To change km/h to m/s, multiply by \( \tfrac{5}{18} \). To change m/s to km/h, multiply by \( \tfrac{18}{5} \). So 72 km/h = 20 m/s and 36 km/h = 10 m/s.

Acceleration

Definition

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time.

Acceleration
\( a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} \)
SI unit: m/s² (m s⁻²) · vector
  • Positive acceleration: velocity increases in the direction of motion.
  • Negative acceleration (retardation or deceleration): velocity decreases, for example when brakes are applied.
  • Uniform acceleration: velocity changes by equal amounts in equal intervals of time, for example a freely falling body.
  • Non-uniform acceleration: velocity changes by unequal amounts in equal intervals of time.

Graphs of motion

Distance–time graph

  • A straight line sloping upwards means uniform speed. The slope of the line gives the speed.
  • A curved line means non-uniform speed.
  • A line parallel to the time axis means the object is at rest.

Velocity–time graph

  • The slope of a velocity–time graph gives the acceleration.
  • The area under the velocity–time graph gives the displacement.
  • A line parallel to the time axis means uniform velocity (zero acceleration).
  • A straight line sloping upwards means uniform acceleration.

Equations of motion

For an object moving in a straight line with uniform acceleration \(a\), starting with velocity \(u\) and reaching velocity \(v\) after time \(t\), having covered displacement \(s\):

First equation (velocity–time)
\( v = u + at \)
Second equation (position–time)
\( s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \)
Third equation (position–velocity)
\( v^2 - u^2 = 2as \)

Derivation from the velocity–time graph

First equation: slope of the graph = acceleration, so \( a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} \), which gives \( v = u + at \).

Second equation: displacement = area under the graph = area of rectangle + area of triangle

\[ s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}\,t\,(v - u) = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}\,t\,(at) = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \]

Third equation: displacement = area of the trapezium \( = \tfrac{1}{2}(u + v)\,t \). Substituting \( t = \dfrac{v - u}{a} \):

\[ s = \frac{(v + u)(v - u)}{2a} \quad\Rightarrow\quad v^2 - u^2 = 2as \]
Remember

“Starts from rest” means \( u = 0 \). “Comes to rest” or “stops” means \( v = 0 \). When the object slows down, \( a \) is negative.

Uniform circular motion

When an object moves along a circular path with constant speed, its motion is called uniform circular motion. The speed is constant, but the direction of motion changes at every point, so the velocity changes. Uniform circular motion is therefore accelerated motion.

Speed in a circle
\( v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{t} \)
r = radius, t = time for one round

Examples: the Moon going around the Earth, a stone tied to a string and whirled in a circle, the tip of a clock’s second hand. When the string breaks, the stone flies off along the tangent to the circle at that point.

Solved examples

A person walks 300 m due east in 2.5 minutes, then turns and walks 100 m due west in 1 minute. Find (a) the average speed and (b) the average velocity.

Total time \( = 3.5\ \text{min} = 210\ \text{s} \).

(a) Distance \( = 300 + 100 = 400\ \text{m} \). Average speed \( = \dfrac{400}{210} = 1.90\ \text{m/s} \).

(b) Displacement \( = 300 - 100 = 200\ \text{m} \) east. Average velocity \( = \dfrac{200}{210} = 0.95\ \text{m/s} \) east.

A bus decreases its speed from 80 km/h to 60 km/h in 5 s. Find the acceleration of the bus.

\( u = 80 \times \tfrac{5}{18} = 22.22\ \text{m/s} \), \( v = 60 \times \tfrac{5}{18} = 16.67\ \text{m/s} \), \( t = 5\ \text{s} \).

\( a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} = \dfrac{16.67 - 22.22}{5} = -1.11\ \text{m/s}^2 \)

The bus has a retardation of 1.11 m/s² (the negative sign shows it is slowing down).

A car starts from rest and moves with a uniform acceleration of 2 m/s² for 10 s. Find the final velocity and the distance covered.

\( u = 0,\ a = 2\ \text{m/s}^2,\ t = 10\ \text{s} \)

\( v = u + at = 0 + 2 \times 10 = 20\ \text{m/s} \)

\( s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 10^2 = 100\ \text{m} \)

Final velocity = 20 m/s; distance = 100 m.

A train moving at 72 km/h is brought to rest by applying brakes that produce a uniform retardation. It stops in 10 s. Find the retardation and the distance travelled before stopping.

\( u = 72 \times \tfrac{5}{18} = 20\ \text{m/s},\ v = 0,\ t = 10\ \text{s} \)

\( a = \dfrac{0 - 20}{10} = -2\ \text{m/s}^2 \)

Using \( v^2 - u^2 = 2as \): \( 0 - 20^2 = 2(-2)s \), so \( s = \dfrac{400}{4} = 100\ \text{m} \).

Retardation = 2 m/s²; stopping distance = 100 m.

A cyclist goes once around a circular track of diameter 140 m in 44 s. Find the speed of the cyclist. (Take \( \pi = \tfrac{22}{7} \).)

Radius \( r = 70\ \text{m} \). Circumference \( = 2\pi r = 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 70 = 440\ \text{m} \).

\( v = \dfrac{440}{44} = 10\ \text{m/s} \)

Speed = 10 m/s. (After one full round the displacement is zero, so the average velocity is zero.)

Quick revision

  • Distance is scalar; displacement is vector. Both are measured in metres.
  • Speed = distance ÷ time; velocity = displacement ÷ time. Unit: m/s.
  • km/h × 5/18 = m/s.
  • Acceleration \( a = (v - u)/t \), unit m/s². Negative acceleration = retardation.
  • Distance–time graph: slope = speed. Velocity–time graph: slope = acceleration, area = displacement.
  • Equations of motion: \( v = u + at \), \( s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2 \), \( v^2 - u^2 = 2as \).
  • From rest: \( u = 0 \). Stops: \( v = 0 \).
  • Uniform circular motion is accelerated motion because the direction changes continuously; \( v = 2\pi r/t \).
